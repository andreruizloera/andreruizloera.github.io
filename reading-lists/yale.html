Yale, Self-Studied

Every book Yale assigns in the 39 courses it gives away free

Open Yale Courses publishes 39 complete courses with their syllabi. Between them they assign 328 texts, listed here by course exactly as Yale lists them. These courses fill 34 of the roughly 70 credits needed for six Yale majors — English, Classics, Philosophy, History, Economics, and Ethics, Politics & Economics. Only Open Yale Courses publishes syllabi; every other Yale syllabus sits behind a login, so nothing in that list is invented to fill a gap. A second list, further down, covers the required courses Open Yale does not reach, and it is marked separately because most of it is reconstructed rather than quoted.

The Other 36 Credits

The required courses Open Yale does not reach

The list above is quoted from Yale. This one is not, and the difference matters more than the books do.

Open Yale covers 34 of the roughly 70 credits behind these six majors. The rest are the courses every major actually requires and nobody publishes: intermediate theory, econometrics, logic, the history-of-philosophy sequence, the language sequences. I went looking for those syllabi on department pages, faculty pages, course catalogues, Reddit and student course sites. Almost none of them are public. Yale's own catalogue gives course descriptions and no reading lists, and the syllabus itself sits on Canvas behind a login.

So each entry below carries one of two marks, and they are not the same kind of claim:

  • syllabus found means a real syllabus for that Yale course is public and linked. Trust it as far as its date.
  • reconstructed means no syllabus was found and these are my best guess at what a course like that assigns, from the course catalogue description and the standard text in the field. It is not what Yale assigns. It is what a course of that name and level usually assigns.

Nothing reconstructed should be quoted as Yale's syllabus, including by me.
