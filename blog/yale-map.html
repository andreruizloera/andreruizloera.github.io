Yale, Self-Studied

August 25, 2026 · 39 courses · 328 texts · 6 majors

What a Yale major actually requires, what Yale gives away free, and every book on the syllabi

Yale has 39 courses online with full syllabi and no paywall, and Yale also publishes exactly what each major requires. So I put the two side by side. Under every major below is the real requirement list out of the Programs of Study, with what the free courses cover marked against it.

The answer is that none of the six is close, and it is the same wall every time. Seminars, senior essays and language courses are not lecture recordings, and those are exactly what the requirements are built out of. Economics wants twelve courses and the free ones reach one of them. Classics dies on two terms of Greek or Latin that do not exist here at any price.

That is still worth knowing. It tells me which parts I can actually get from Yale for nothing and which parts I have to get somewhere else.

    Every text on the syllabi

    Same thing course by course. Rows are courses, columns are majors, two dots means it counts twice. The thirteen below the line count for nothing. A dot here means the course sits in that department, not that it satisfies a requirement. The requirement tables under each major are the ones that answer that.

    The requirement lists come from the Yale College Programs of Study 2026 to 2027 and each one links back to its page. Titles, text counts and links for the free courses come from the Open Yale Courses syllabi.

    The covered, partly and nothing marks are my reading of how a free course lines up against a requirement, and a director of undergraduate studies would rule differently on some of them. Two things I checked rather than assumed: Yale renumbered its courses, so the ECON 159 on Open Yale is ECON 2259 in the current catalog and still Polak's, and the other two economics courses, Geanakoplos on Financial Theory and Shiller on Financial Markets, are not in the catalog at all any more.

    The writing under each heading is mine and it is opinion.

    The 39 free courses assign 328 texts between them. This is all of them, by course, exactly as the syllabi list them. Open Yale is the only part of Yale that publishes syllabi at all; every other one sits behind a login, so nothing here is filled in from guesswork.
